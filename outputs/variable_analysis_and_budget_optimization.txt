==============================================================================
AGE, THE FULL STORY: marginal vs conditional (the flagship test)
==============================================================================

--- 1) Marginal vs conditional correlations ---
  cor(age, value)              MARGINAL   =  0.029
  cor(age, spend)              spend-link =  0.012
  cor(age, value-per-dollar)   CONDITIONAL=  0.444
Reading it: The marginal age-to-value link is tiny (0.029) yet the conditional link is large
  (0.444). The reason: age carries almost no spend information (age-to-spend is 0.012), so age is
  not redundant with spend and its signal only surfaces once you hold spend fixed, that is, in the
  value-per-dollar ratio.


--- 2) Incremental R-squared of adding age to the spend model ---
  R2  value ~ spend          = 0.99848
  R2  value ~ spend + age    = 0.99875
  incremental R2 from age    = 0.00027
  age coefficient            = $1.41 per year
Reading it: Spend already explains 99.8% of value. Adding age lifts that by only 0.00027 of R2
  (about $1.41 per year of age). Statistically detectable, but practically negligible.


--- 3) Value returned per $1 of past spend, by age band ---
  18-28   $0.93 returned per $1 spent
  29-38   $1.02 returned per $1 spent
  39-48   $1.11 returned per $1 spent
  49-58   $1.16 returned per $1 spent
  59+     $1.23 returned per $1 spent
Reading it: The return per dollar rises steadily with age, from about $0.93 for the youngest band
  to about $1.23 for the oldest. Older customers give back a little more per dollar they have
  already spent.


--- 4) Within-segment age effect (young age<=38 vs old age>=59) ---
  segment                  corr      young        old      gap
  VIPs                   -0.026     $3,825     $3,623    $-202
  Bargain Hunters         0.398       $330       $417      $87
  Occasional Shoppers     0.334       $144       $197      $53
Reading it: The age tilt is real inside Bargain Hunters and Occasional Shoppers: older members are
  worth more, a positive correlation and a positive old-minus-young gap. For VIPs the correlation
  is essentially zero to negative and the gap flips negative, so among top customers age gives no
  edge, if anything the younger VIPs are worth more.


--- 5) ANOVA of value across age bands ---
  aov(value ~ age band)  p-value = 0.633
Reading it: The p-value is 0.633, far above 0.05, so total value does not differ across age bands.
  Once you look at the raw dollars per customer, age bands are flat.

Reading it: Age is a WEAK second-order retention signal: among equal-spend mid and low value
  customers, older ones return a little more per dollar, but it adds almost nothing to the value
  model (+ 0.0003 R2) and it VANISHES for VIPs, and total value is flat across age bands (ANOVA not
  significant, p= 0.63). So age is NOT a targeting lever, at most a minor within-segment
  tiebreaker, and never for VIPs.

KEY TAKEAWAYS: one-look summary of everything above
==============================================================================
  1. Value is concentrated: 148 VIPs (14.8% of customers) hold 72.4% of next-year value.
  2. Value is driven by behaviour, not demographics: spend correlates 0.999 with value and alone predicts 99.8% of it;
     age correlates only 0.029 and is a weak within-segment retention tiebreaker that does not apply to VIPs.
  3. The acquisition mix is net positive overall: about $617,763 after commission and signup cost.
  4. Best coupon plan: VIPs $20, Bargain Hunters $5, Occasional Shoppers $10, returning $14,450 net for $9,750 spent.
